StickTime is a real 3D FPV quad simulator that runs on your EdgeTX radio. Download **StickTime-1.7.1-sdcard.zip** below and copy what your radio needs from its `SCRIPTS/TOOLS/` folder to `/SCRIPTS/TOOLS/` on the radio's SD card, then start it from **SYS → Tools**:

- **Color radios:** `StickTime.lua`, and the `StickTimeGhosts` and `StickTimeTracks` folders
- **B&W radios with an STM32F4** (TX12 MkII, Zorro, Boxer, Pocket, X9D+ 2019, X7 ACCESS, T20...): `StickTimeBW.lua`, the `StickTimeBW` folder and the `StickTimeTracks` folder
- **Older B&W radios** (STM32F2: X7, X9D, X9D+, X-Lite, TX12 MkI...): `StickTimeLite.lua` and the `StickTimeLite` folder

The B&W versions need EdgeTX 2.11 or newer (2.11 is the last EdgeTX for STM32F2 radios). The color version also runs on older EdgeTX. **When updating**, replace the whole `StickTimeBW` folder; replace the `StickTimeLite` folder but keep its `data.txt` (your Lite saves).

> The radio keeps transmitting your sticks while the sim runs: unplug the quad's battery or switch the RF module off first.

**Or let the browser do it:** in Chrome or Edge on a computer, open https://juricabi.github.io/sticktime/, plug the radio in by USB, choose USB Storage on the radio, then click **Install**, **Connect radio** and **Install / update**. It writes the editions you pick, brings an older version up to date, removes old files the release doesn't have and leaves your saves, tracks and ghosts alone. Eject the drive before you unplug the radio.

**Try it first in your browser:** https://juricabi.github.io/sticktime/

### New in 1.7.1
- **The next gate's marker turns green when you're lined up** (the color edition): the small triangle over the next gate goes from yellow to green when you're on its way-in side, within 70° of its axis, and heading within 70° of the way through. A flag is a turn: it's green while you're before it on the side you pass it (its pole's free side), whichever way you head; a dive gate needs only that you're above it. Coming from behind or from far to the side, or turned away, it stays yellow.
- **Track gallery:** `tracks/` in the repo holds tracks anyone can fly, starting with the MultiGP qualifier tracks of 2017 to 2026, built from MultiGP's published diagrams (scaled 1.7x to the game's gates) and each checked again against them. Fly one in the emulator (**Tracks** → **Open**, under Gallery), or copy its `.txt` to `StickTimeTracks/` on the radio as `c1.txt` … `c8.txt`. Made a good track? Add it with a pull request: `tracks/README.md` says how, and a check flies it in the emulator.
- **Browser emulator, the track editor reworked**, every button with one job:
  - **Open** picks a track to edit: your tracks (the emulator's slots and the radio's), a new one (blank or a copy of a built-in track), one from the gallery, or a file.
  - **My tracks** shows the slots, c1 to c8 (tracks 9 to 16), in the emulator and on the radio side by side: **Remove** a track, copy one slot across (→, ←) or all of them, and see where a gallery track you have has a newer version (**Update**).
  - Under the map, **To slot** picks where the track goes and never changes it; then **Send to emulator**, **Fly c1** (flies that slot of the emulator), **Send to radio** and **Save file**, each with what that slot holds there. The head says what you're editing (a copy, kept in the browser as you edit) and whether a slot has it; a slot with an earlier version of your track is said in yellow, and **Fly** asks before flying it as it is. **Send to radio** never makes a second copy of a track the radio has in another slot.
  - **Connect radio** in the editor first says what to do on the radio (USB Storage, then pick its drive).
- **Install** is the connection and the game only, with a new **Faster firmware** part: where to get the fast-lua build and how to flash it (EdgeTX's manual).
- For developers: `tools/gallery.py` checks `tracks/` and writes the site's `tracks/index.json`; `test/gallery_test.py` opens and flies every gallery track in the emulator (the **Track gallery** workflow runs it on pull requests); the headless tests cover the green marker.

### New in 1.7.0
- **Hits don't end the flight any more.** Gates, poles, trees, walls and the ground are as solid as before, but the quad stops against them (no bounce) and slides on instead of respawning. A hit at 1.5 m/s or more with the props or arms edge-on is a prop strike: for up to half a second (longer the harder the hit) the props lose most of their thrust and the flight controller its hold, so the quad tumbles about the point that touched, and you fly it out, as with a real quad; a level landing doesn't strike. A scrape along a wall costs a little speed; a head-on hit at racing speed stops you dead. You can land on roofs and containers. On its back or side on the ground the props can't lift the quad: **turtle mode**, roll or pitch to flip it back over. Only flying off the world still puts you back by itself; the pause menu has a new **Respawn** (back at the last gate, with a fresh battery pack). In Freestyle a hit or a Respawn ends the combo, and a tumble counts as no trick.
- **Medals.** On every track: **Gold**, **Silver** and **Bronze** are the lap times of the Hard, Medium and Easy AI pilot at the Power you fly. Your best lap is judged against them (whichever quad set it): the color menu shows your medal next to the track's best lap; Practice shows the next medal's lap time on the OSD (B&W: at the top, and in the menu on 212×64 screens); a new best lap that earns a better medal says so; the race results show the medal of the race's best lap.
- **Battery** (setting, Off by default): **Real**, **1.5x** or **2x** (one and a half or twice the capacity). Each quad flies its own pack (4S 1300 mAh on the racer, 4S 1500 on the freestyler, 3S 550 LiHV on the 3", 1S and 2S 300 LiHV on the whoops) that sags under load and runs down: about 2 minutes of hard racing on the racer, 4.6 cruising. A fresh pack flies exactly like the quad without it (Betaflight's sag compensation), so no lap gets faster; as it drains, the punches fade; more Power draws more current. Below 3.5 V a cell a beep every 2 s, below 3.3 V a double beep. The color OSD shows a small battery that empties and the cell voltage, yellow and then red and blinking (no big warning text); B&W shows the cell voltage and a small gauge, inverted and blinking, and LAND. Near empty the quad limps; once full throttle can't lift more than 1.25 times the weight the motors stop: EMPTY, and you respawn with a fresh pack. Every start and the pause menu's **Respawn** bring a fresh pack too; the pause and finish screens show the flight time and mAh used.
- **Ghost** (the color edition, setting **Ghost**, on by default): in Practice your fastest practice lap on the track flies along with you, as a white quad, from each lap's start. It is saved on the SD card (`StickTimeGhosts/c<track>.txt`), one per track. StickTime BW leaves it out, even on a color radio: it would take most of the memory the smallest B&W radios have left.
- **New layouts for the four oval tracks.** **Meadow** is a kidney with a long start straight, a dent the line swings into and an S on the way home; **Dive Tower** climbs through a 10 m high gate, rounds a hoop and dives through the dive gate; **Hoop Forest** weaves through the trees in S-bends with hoops from 2 to 6 m; **Grand Prix** has a long main straight, a flag hairpin, a hoop chicane, a second flag hairpin, the dive gate and a fast back section with an 8 m high gate. Their lap, race and Gate Rush bests start over (combo scores stay). StickTime Lite gets them too.
- **Your own tracks:** tracks 9 to 16 are files on the SD card (`StickTimeTracks/c1.txt` to `c8.txt`), read by StickTime and StickTime BW and listed by name after Pro Track. Make them in the browser emulator's new **track editor** (**Tracks**): lay out gates, hoops, flags, dive gates, structures and walls with gaps on a map, starting from any of the eight tracks or a blank one; it checks that the game can take the track, then **Fly it** in the emulator, **Save file**, or, in Chrome or Edge with the radio plugged in as USB Storage, **Send to radio**. The **Install** button shows the tracks on the radio, opens one in the editor, and copies the tracks you have either way. A different track in a slot starts that slot's bests and ghost over. Restart StickTime on the radio to see a new track.
- **Install / update StickTime from the emulator** (the **Install** button, on the hosted site; Chrome or Edge on a computer, the radio plugged in as USB Storage): it writes the editions you pick (Color, B&W, Lite), removes old files the release doesn't have in those folders, and never touches your saves, tracks or ghosts.
- **Saving:** a new best or ghost is written the moment you set it, so switching the radio off right after keeps it. A race never writes the ghost; in Practice, the lap that sets a new ghost writes about 5 KB, which can show as a short pause on the radio.
- **More memory for StickTime BW:** the test hooks the emulator and the tests use are no longer in the B&W game file. On the smallest B&W radio (113.6 KB heap) it peaks at 100.8 KB with every track and a biggest custom track, about 12 KB to spare.
- **Browser emulator:** **Tracks** and **Install** buttons on top, a tidier toolbar that fits one row on a desktop, and a thud on hits in the motor sound.
- StickTime Lite: the new layouts only; it has no memory for the rest, and a hit is still a crash there.
- For developers: `build.py`'s `--#if` blocks nest and may name several builds (`--#if TEST` marks the test hooks; StickTime BW gets a test build in `test/build/`); the headless tests cover medals against the AI, wall scrapes, head-on hits, landings, turtle mode, Respawn, the ghost (record, replace, read back, Off), the battery (sag, warnings, EMPTY, Respawn, 2x, pausing), custom tracks (including hand-made files the editor wouldn't write) and the save layouts; `test/editor_test.py` covers the track editor, the radio's SD card and the installer; the memory test drives the shipped B&W game through its menus over every track and a biggest custom track; StickTime Lite passes on all 13 heaps.

### New in 1.6.7
- **Re-released 2026-10-13** with a fix and one more flight model change, so download the zip again. The fix: the first 1.6.7 zip gave StickTime Lite's **Whoop 65mm** wrong numbers (one value was written as `5.9e-12`, which the Lite read as two, so every number after it was shifted); the color and B&W versions were not affected. The change: flips shift the thrust like an airmode mixer (below).
- **The flight model, refitted closer to the detailed per-prop model it comes from.** Every class now lands within 0.01 of its hover stick, about 2 km/h of its punch-out and top speed and 1 km/h of its flat fall, and steady flight at any pitch and throttle is within 0.2-0.5 m/s on average (it was up to 0.9). [docs/flight-model.md](../flight-model.md) has the numbers.
  - **Throttle:** each class's curve now follows the model's from low throttle up. It used to be fitted where the thrust is big, so a high-power quad sat about 10% low around hover. The Racer hovers at 0.33 and gets the model's bottom dead zone (about 12% of the stick); the top of the stick is a little steeper.
  - **Translational lift levels off by about 15 m/s** instead of growing on: a 5" at hover throttle gains about +20% thrust at 6 m/s and +45% at 15 m/s (it was +28% and +64%), so it balloons less when you level out at speed. The gain also grows with throttle before it fades at full stick.
  - **Fast drops:** at idle the blades stall, with power they hold their thrust. Flat falls match on every class.
  - **Flips shift the thrust the way Betaflight's airmode mixer does.** While a full-stick flip spins up, the motors need a differential on top of the throttle: at full throttle the high side can't go any higher, so the thrust dips and a full-throttle flip ends lower; near hover the low side can't go below idle, so the quad lifts a little. The rotation itself is as quick and clean as before (no overshoot); only the thrust changes. One new class number, `am`, fitted to the per-prop model's thrust while a flip starts: a full-stick flip now ends within 0.1-0.6 m of the model's height (it was 0.4-1.0 m).
  - Slides, Grip, the prop wash strengths and the rates are unchanged.
- **More memory headroom on B&W radios.** The loaders' "why the game didn't load" screen is a file of its own now, `why.lua` in the game's folder, so its code is not in memory while the game loads; the loader also frees what compiling it left behind before it loads the game, and the Lite frees memory before it builds a track. StickTime Lite's peak on an STM32F2 radio is 59.0 KB (it was 60.8), so it runs on a heap 2.8 KB smaller than the radio's; StickTime BW needs about 3 KB less. **When updating, replace the whole `StickTimeBW` / `StickTimeLite` folder.**
- For developers: four new class numbers in every edition (`tk` the throttle curve's steep top end, `tb` the lift's growth with throttle, `rq` the stall floor in a fast drop, `am` the flips' mixer), in `tools/tune_physics.py` (with a flip test) and the docs too; the test autopilots invert the new throttle curve, the headless tests check that flips shift the thrust, `build.py` refuses an exponent in the class numbers, and the memory test passes from 60.5 KB of heap up.

### New in 1.6.6
- **Faster drawing on color radios, same looks.** The sky and ground are painted once instead of twice (`lcd.clear()` paints the larger of the two), rolled views need fewer triangle rows, the minimap's building marks are plain fills instead of see-through ones (each see-through fill is a slow call on the radio), shapes are clipped only against the screen edges they cross, and the draw order is worked out per track instead of every frame, starting from the last frame's order. On busy tracks (Bando, around the tower) the game does up to 16% less Lua work per frame; diving and fast flight fill up to 40% fewer pixels. StickTime BW and Lite are unchanged.
- **Browser emulator:** a **Full HD PC screen** (1920×1080 at 60 fps) next to the 1280×720 one.

### New in 1.6.5
- **A new flight model, fitted class by class.** Five quads now: **Racer 5"** (0.49 kg, 7.7:1), **Freestyle 5"** (an HD freestyler, 0.66 kg, 6.9:1), **3" toothpick**, **Whoop 65mm** (1S) and **Whoop 75mm** (a 47 g ducted 2S whoop, 3.5:1), each with its own throttle curve and idle, drag, ground effect and prop wash, measured from a detailed per-prop model (blade-element props on rpm-governed motors, a Betaflight-style PID loop - see [docs/flight-model.md](../flight-model.md)). The **Power** setting gains **Stock** (the class's own thrust/weight) and offers each quad the range that suits it: 5-12:1 on the racer, 4-10:1 on the freestyler, 3-10:1 on the 3", 3-8:1 on the whoops (a Power the new quad doesn't have goes back to Stock).
- The physics the classes were fitted to: translational lift (the same throttle gives up to 50% more thrust edgewise), a descent cushion (sinking gains thrust ~2.5x faster than climbing loses it), frame downforce when nose-down at speed, more drag at low speed, ground effect within a twentieth of the prop diameter (+17% on a whoop skimming the floor, +44% on a 3"), motors that spool in ~6 ms, and **no rotor drag** - like the model it was fitted to, a 5" slide halves in about 4 s (the new **Grip** setting adds it). Top speed, punch-out, idle fall, hover stick and slide times all land within a few percent of the measurements for every class.
- **Prop wash** is now Off / **Stock** / **Max**: each class's own measured strength at Stock (a whisper on the whoop, light on the 3"), the full-strength set at Max. It costs thrust when you sink through your own downwash (the mush you feel), and shakes the quad the way a real one does, measured from real flight logs: tenths of a degree at the class's own frequency (20-45 Hz), worst with the throttle chopped, each wash a little different; edgewise speed clears it.
- **Motor sound in the browser emulator** (the radio still can't do better than a plain tone): four synthesized motors, each with its own rpm that follows your stick inputs, tuned against recordings of real quads - a nearly pure blade-pass tone over a soft air whoosh (no hiss), shaft growl and electrical whine (the whoop screams at 4-6.6 kHz), a steady pitch, panned left and right, beating like real props; a dark rumble descending into your own wash, the motors' chatter in prop wash, an air-rush voice that grows with speed, a thud on crashes, a swell on spool-up, shaped per class. The **Sound** slider (0 is off) now sets the beeps' volume too, on a loudness curve, with a mute button and mouse-wheel steps; the keyboard / gamepad choice is remembered.
- **Music in the browser emulator**: melodic electronic music composed live (free, nothing to download): every song has its own key, tempo, seventh-chord progression and hook, arranged like a real track with a filtered intro, build, drop and breakdown, or **Songs** to play your own files (**×** goes back to the generated music); its own switch, level and next-song button.
- **Grip** setting: **Low** (the fitted model's full momentum), **Medium** (the default) or **High** (rotor drag: props moving edgewise are pushed back, so the quad carves and stops sooner; a racer's slide halves in 4, 3 or 2 s). On every radio, the Lite too.
- The **Bando**'s tower is taller (36 m): room for orbits, reverse orbits and dives.
- AI pilots, autopilot and the tests use each class's own throttle curve.
- **When updating, replace the whole `StickTimeBW` / `StickTimeLite` folder**: the Lite's quads are files in its folder now (`q1.txt` to `q5.txt`).
- For developers: `tools/tune_physics.py` checks every class; the headless tests cover all five classes and the three prop-wash settings. About the same CPU as 1.6.4 (slightly fewer instructions per frame on average) and +2 KB of memory.

### New in 1.6.4
- StickTime BW and Lite no longer risk a crash at start. Their precompiled game is now `game.luac`, and the loader loads only that. It used to be `core.luac` next to its source `core.lua`, and EdgeTX compiles `core.lua` on the radio whenever that file looks newer, which a copy that does not keep file times can cause. A B&W radio does not have the memory to compile the game (it takes about 200 KB), and running out of it could crash the radio. **When updating, replace the whole `StickTimeBW` / `StickTimeLite` folder.**
- When the game can't start, the loader says what to do on a screen of its own: **Needs EdgeTX 2.11 or newer** on EdgeTX 2.10 and older, **Not enough memory on this radio. Use StickTime Lite.** for StickTime BW on an STM32F2 radio, or **Copy the whole folder** when the folder is missing. EXIT (RTN) closes it. EdgeTX's own error box showed a 128×64 radio only the end of these messages.
- For developers: `tools/build_etxhost.sh v2.10.7` builds EdgeTX 2.10's Lua for the memory tests, and the tests check the loader's screen on it.
